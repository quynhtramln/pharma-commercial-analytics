# 06 · Insights & Recommendations

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Synthetic data.** The numbers below come from a generated dataset. They show how the analysis is

**Period:** 1 Jan 2023 to 29 Jun 2026 (the as-of date), unless a section says otherwise.
**Rule used throughout:** engagement and sales are compared at account level. A link between them is
an association, not proof that engagement causes sales (Business Rule 8).

## 1 · Sales performance: is sales going up or down, and what drives it? (Q1)

**What we see**
- Sales total **$482.2M**: $137.8M in 2023, $138.5M in 2024, $137.6M in 2025 and $68.3M in H1 2026.
  That is **+0.6 %** on H1 2025.
- Monthly sales sit around $11.5M and move by only ±3 %. There is no seasonal pattern.
- The small H1 increase (+$0.4M) is the net of 23 products going up (+$0.9M) and 17 going down
  (-$0.5M). By therapeutic area, Oncology (+$179K), Diabetes (+$174K) and Cardiology (+$115K) went up,
  while CNS (-$104K) and Dermatology (-$37K) went down.
- North brings **41.9 %** of sales, South 29.3 %, Central 19.4 % and Mekong 9.4 %. Mekong grows fastest
  (+2.1 % YoY in H1).
- 7.5 % of sales (about $36M) has no therapeutic area because 3 products are missing it in master data.

**What it means:** growth is not coming from a few big drivers. It is many small gains and losses that
almost cancel out. Without a deliberate push, next year will look like this year.

**What to do**
- Set a growth target per territory and per therapeutic area, and review progress every month.
- Use Mekong as a test territory: find out what is working there before copying it to other territories.
- Add the missing therapeutic area for the 3 products so that all sales can be reported.

*Owner: Commercial leadership, Product master data · Track: YoY sales by territory and therapeutic area*

## 2 · Account growth: which accounts grow and which decline? (Q2)

**What we see**
- Last 90 days vs the 90 days before: **+0.1 %** overall.
- In H1, 577 accounts grew and 523 declined. The top 50 growing accounts make up only **22 %** of all
  gains, so growth is broad, not concentrated.
- Pharmacy accounts grew **+4.2 %**, while Wholesaler fell **-2.1 %** and Distributor fell -0.7 %.
- **81 of the largest accounts** (top quarter by sales) are in the *Declining* tier. Together they sold
  **$11.1M** in the last 12 months (8 % of sales). 34 of them are in North.

**What it means:** there is no small group of "key accounts" carrying the business. The bigger risk is
the large accounts that are slowly shrinking, because losing them costs more than any single win brings in.

**What to do**
- Make a watch-list of the 81 large declining accounts. Ask the account owner for the reason within
  one month: lost tender, stock issue, competitor, or price.
- Treat the three tiers differently: protect *Growing* accounts, find the cause in *Declining*
  accounts, and look for upsell in *Stable* accounts.
- Review wholesaler terms and stock, since that channel is the one shrinking.

*Owner: Key Account Management, Trade · Track: number of large accounts in Declining; sales of the watch-list*

## 3 · Product momentum: which products grow and which lose momentum? (Q7)

**What we see**
- 14 products are *Rising*, 13 *Stable* and 13 *Declining*. Momentum ranges from -8.4 % to +7.3 %.
- **6 of the 10 priority products are declining**, 3 are stable and only 1 is rising. Among the 30
  other products, 13 are rising.
- All products sell a similar amount ($3.4M to $3.6M each in the last 12 months), so size and momentum
  have to be read together:
  - **High sales, declining:** 6 products, $21.0M (15 % of sales), 2 of them priority products. These
    protect today's base.
  - **Lower sales, rising:** 9 products, $30.7M. These are candidates to grow.
- Oncology fell **-2.1 %** in the last 90 days, with 4 of its 7 products declining. Dermatology sales
  are up +1.2 %, but engagement on it is down -2.7 %.

**What it means:** the products the company calls "priority" are the ones losing ground. Looking only at
which product sells most would miss this.

**What to do**
- Hold a short review for each of the 6 declining priority products: price, competitor activity,
  hospital listing and message. Agree one fix per product.
- Keep the high-sales declining products stable (defend) and test more support on 2–3 of the rising
  lower-sales products (grow).
- Watch Dermatology: falling engagement often shows up in sales later.

*Owner: Brand / Marketing · Track: momentum tier of priority products each quarter*

## 4 · HCP engagement: how engaged are HCPs? (Q3)

**What we see**
- **298,769** interactions over the period. All 5,200 HCPs were contacted, about 1.9 times a month.
- In the last 12 months, most HCPs had 20 to 26 interactions (range 9 to 43).
- Among active HCPs: 2,182 were contacted in the last 30 days, 310 in the last 31–60 days, 43 in the
  last 61–90 days and 8 more than 90 days ago.
- **418 active HCPs** had at least 50 % fewer contacts in the last 90 days than in the 90 days before.
- **51 % of interactions** in the last 12 months went to HCPs whose status is *Inactive*, and **54 %**
  were logged by reps whose status is *Inactive*.
- Priority products get **24.9 %** of interactions, which is simply their share of the portfolio. The
  engagement score is 5.5 for every channel and specialty.

**What it means:** on the surface engagement looks healthy, but the status fields do not match the
activity. Either the statuses are out of date, or activity is being recorded against the wrong people.
Until this is fixed, every "active HCP" number is unreliable. The score also cannot tell a good call
from a weak one.

**What to do**
- Ask CRM / master data to refresh HCP and rep status within one month, and to block new interactions
  on inactive records.
- Split active HCPs into three groups: *keep* (high and steady), *win back* (the 418 whose contacts
  dropped by half), and *reach* (the 51 not contacted in 60+ days).
- Set a target share of calls for priority products (for example 40 %) and rewrite the scoring guide
  so each score level has a clear definition.

*Owner: CRM admin, Sales Excellence, Medical · Track: % of interactions on active records; priority share of calls*

## 5 · Field coverage: is the field team spread the right way? (Q5)

**What we see**
- Every active HCP was contacted at least once, and 99.6 % in the last 90 days. Contact intensity is the
  same in every territory (about 23 interactions per active HCP a year).
- Only **101 of 220 reps** are active. Per active rep:

| Territory | Active reps | Active HCPs per rep | Sales per rep (12 months) | Opportunity accounts per rep |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| North | 33 | **31.6** | **$1.76M** | **3.2** |
| South | 37 | 20.6 | $1.09M | 2.4 |
| Central | 21 | 23.3 | $1.27M | 2.8 |
| Mekong | 10 | 24.5 | $1.30M | 2.3 |

**What it means:** "coverage" is not the issue, because everyone is already reached. The issue is load.
North reps carry about 50 % more HCPs and sales than South reps, and North also has the most
opportunity accounts.

**What to do**
- Move about 6–7 reps' worth of capacity from South to North (through territory changes or new hires), and
  check the effect after one quarter.
- Replace "% of HCPs reached at least once" with "% of target HCPs reached at the planned frequency".
  This needs an HCP segment (A/B/C) and a rep-to-HCP assignment list.

*Owner: Field Force Management · Track: HCPs and sales per active rep by territory*

## 6 · Events: do events create engagement? (Q6)

**What we see**
- Events are similar in size (about 1,030 to 1,240 registrations each), and attendance is about 25 % no
  matter how big the event is.
- HCPs who attended were contacted **1.31 times** in the 30 days after the event, the same as HCPs who
  did not show up (1.31) and almost the same as before the event (1.28).
- For events already held, only 24.6 % of registrations are marked *Attended*. Another 24.6 % are still
  *Registered*, 24.4 % *Cancelled* and 1.8 % blank. Of 534 past events, **200 are still marked Planned**
  and 187 were cancelled.
- 4,687 registrations are marked *Attended* for events that have not happened yet (ISS-066).

**What it means:** right now we cannot say which events work, because half the outcomes are missing and
nothing changes after an event. Counting registrations alone would give a false picture.

**What to do**
- Close each event in the CRM within 7 days: final attendance and event status.
- Add a standard follow-up: a rep contact within 14 days for every attendee, linked to the event.
- After one quarter, measure registration → attendance → follow-up → next interaction per event type,
  and keep the formats that move people along.

*Owner: Medical Affairs / Events, CRM admin · Track: % of registrations with a final status; follow-up within 14 days*

## 7 · Engagement vs sales: does high engagement go with high sales? (Q4)

**What we see**
- **274 accounts** (25 %) have above-median engagement but below-median sales. Another **273 accounts**
  have below-median engagement but above-median sales.
- High-engagement accounts have about **twice as many linked HCPs** (7.4 vs 3.8 on average). Contacts per
  account in the last 90 days are about the same across all groups.
- The 273 low-engagement / high-sales accounts bring **25.6 % of sales** with only **16.4 % of the
  engagement**. 84 of them are declining.

**What it means:** the "engagement" measure is mostly counting how many HCPs are attached to an account,
not how much attention the account gets. The data does not show that more engagement leads to more
sales, and the project does not claim it.

**What to do**
- Rank accounts on engagement per linked HCP, so that large accounts do not look "engaged" just because
  they are large.
- For high engagement / low sales accounts, check what blocks the sale (formulary listing, budget,
  distributor stock) before adding more visits.
- Contact the 84 declining low-engagement / high-sales accounts first. They are the sales most at risk.

*Owner: Key Account Management · Track: sales of the 84 at-risk accounts; engagement per HCP*

## 8 · Opportunity segments: where is the opportunity? (Q8)

| Segment | Accounts | Share of sales | Share of engagement | Meaning | Action |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| High engagement, high sales | 277 | 26.0 % | 33.7 % | Core | Keep and protect |
| Low engagement, high sales | 273 | 25.6 % | 16.4 % | Sales without much contact | Check the risk, focus on the 84 declining |
| High engagement, low sales | 274 | 24.1 % | 33.1 % | Engaged but under-selling | Find the barrier, pilot on the top 50 |
| Low engagement, low sales | 276 | 24.3 % | 16.8 % | Low priority | Low-cost channels only |

**What we see:** lifting all 274 under-selling accounts to the median would add about **$3.6M**
(0.7 % of sales). The top 50 of them hold about **$1.5M** of that. At HCP level, 768 HCPs sit in these
accounts, but **2,846 HCPs (55 %)** have no active primary account, so they cannot be placed in any segment.

**What it means:** the opportunity is real but small, and it is concentrated in a few accounts. Spreading
effort across all 274 would waste time.

**What to do**
- Run a 3-month pilot on the top 50 accounts by value at stake, with a similar group left as it is for
  comparison.
- Ask Commercial Operations to complete the primary account for the 2,846 unlinked HCPs.
- Re-run the segments every quarter and track which accounts move between groups.

*Owner: Commercial Operations, Key Account Management · Track: pilot vs comparison group sales; % of HCPs with a primary account*

## 9 · Data quality: can the data be trusted for decisions? (Q9)

**What we see**
- **72.9 %** of 1.93M fact rows have no open data-quality flag. By table: registrations 50.2 %,
  sales 82.4 %, interactions 87.3 %, affiliations 92.7 %, campaigns 92.8 %.
- Biggest problems: registration recorded after the event started (48.9 %), sale dated before the
  product's launch (15.9 %), and interaction dated before the rep's hire date (10.5 %).
- Rows removed from Total Sales by quality rules are only **0.04 %** of sales value.
- Found in this review: about 48,000 sales, 21,000 interactions and 30,000 registrations are dated
  **after** the as-of date (ISS-065), and the HCP and rep status fields do not match the activity (see section 4).
- How the 67 logged issues were handled:

| Treatment | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Fix** | Clear technical errors corrected in staging (duplicates, placeholders, bad dates, case variants) or confirmed business errors corrected in master with an audit column |
| **Quarantine** | Records pointing to customers or products that do not exist, kept aside and never guessed |
| **Review** (flag) | Values kept as they are and flagged until the business owner confirms the cause |
| **Accept** | Confirmed valid, for example optional fields left empty |

**What it means:** the answer depends on the question. Sales totals, mix and trends can be used. Anything
based on dates (event timing, before/after launch, rep tenure), statuses or event outcomes needs care.

**What to do**
- Fix the problems at source: registration time in the CRM, product launch dates, rep hire dates, and
  HCP and rep status. Do not patch values quietly in reports.
- Check future dates against the as-of date, not today's date. Rule BR-049 compares with `GETDATE()`,
  and that is how ISS-065 was missed.
- Use the Data quality page as a release check: no report goes out while a Critical issue is open.

*Owner: Data owner, CRM admin, Master data · Track: quality score by table (target 95 %); open Critical/High issues*

## Note on the data

The dataset is synthetic. Some measures are almost perfectly even: attendance is 25 % everywhere,
channels are one third each, and products and accounts are of similar size. That comes from how the
data was generated. In a real company, a measure that does not change between groups is a finding in
itself: it means the measure is not capturing what it was built to capture. The recommendations above
are written with that in mind.
